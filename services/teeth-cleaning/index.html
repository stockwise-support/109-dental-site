Patient guide

Teeth cleaning and hygiene visits

Understand what a hygiene appointment includes and how it differs from treatment for gum disease.

What does a cleaning do?

Professional cleaning removes plaque and hardened deposits that brushing alone cannot remove. The visit also gives you a chance to discuss bleeding gums, difficult-to-clean areas, and your home routine.

Is a cleaning the same as a checkup?

No. A checkup assesses oral health; cleaning removes deposits. They can be booked together, but are separate parts of care. Ask what your appointment includes and whether the dentist will see you that day.

What if my gums bleed or my teeth are sensitive?

Tell the dental team before cleaning starts. Bleeding can be a sign of gum inflammation and deserves assessment. Gum disease may require care below the gum line and a different follow-up schedule from routine cleaning.

How much time and money should I plan for?

The amount of cleaning needed affects appointment length and cost. If it has been a while since your last visit, say so when booking. Ask for an estimate and whether additional visits could be needed.

Between appointments

Brush with fluoride toothpaste and clean between teeth daily. Ask the hygienist to demonstrate an approach that works around your bridges, braces or other dental work. Your return interval should reflect your needs, rather than a fixed schedule for everyone.

Planning a visit to 109 Dental

Call (780) 435-5300 to ask which appointment to book and whether the care you need is available at the clinic. Bring any referral, recent dental records, medication list, and coverage details. An assessment helps determine the next step, including whether a referral is needed.
