Patient guide

Dental bridges for missing teeth

A bridge fills a gap left by missing teeth and is supported by teeth or implants.

How does a bridge work?

A bridge joins replacement teeth to supports on either side of a gap. A conventional tooth-supported bridge usually requires preparation of the supporting teeth. The dentist checks those teeth, the gums and your bite before recommending it.

Bridge, implant or denture?

A bridge is fixed in place. A removable partial denture can replace missing teeth without the same design, while an implant-supported option uses support placed in the jaw. Each involves different treatment, cleaning and cost considerations. Not every option suits every mouth.

What does treatment involve?

Planning may include images and records, care for the supporting teeth, preparation and fitting. Ask whether you will have a temporary restoration, how many appointments are expected and what to do if it becomes loose.

Cleaning under a bridge

The replacement tooth and supporting teeth need daily cleaning. Ask the team to demonstrate a floss threader or another suitable aid for reaching beneath the bridge.

Planning the cost

The size of the bridge and the condition of its supports affect the plan. Request an itemised estimate, including any care required first, and check coverage before proceeding.

Planning a visit to 109 Dental

Call (780) 435-5300 to ask which appointment to book and whether the care you need is available at the clinic. Bring any referral, recent dental records, medication list, and coverage details. An assessment helps determine the next step, including whether a referral is needed.
