Patient guide

Infant tongue-tie and feeding concerns

Feeding difficulties need an assessment of the baby and feeding process, not just the appearance of a tongue-tie.

Does every tongue-tie need treatment?

No. Some babies with tongue-tie feed without difficulty. If feeding is painful, prolonged or ineffective, seek help from the baby's medical provider and a qualified feeding professional. An examination can help establish whether tongue movement is contributing.

What happens before a procedure is considered?

Assessment should consider feeding, growth and other possible causes of the difficulty. Feeding support may be part of the plan. A visible band of tissue alone does not establish that a frenectomy will help.

Questions about a referral

Tell the office your baby's age, the feeding concern and who referred you. Confirm whether infant assessment or treatment is currently available and who would provide it. Bring relevant referral notes and feeding assessments.

Benefits, risks and follow-up

Ask what current problem the procedure is intended to address, what improvement is realistic, what risks are involved, and what alternatives exist. Do not assume it will prevent future speech, dental or developmental problems. Arrange a clear follow-up and feeding-support plan with the treating team.

Planning a visit to 109 Dental

Call (780) 435-5300 to ask which appointment to book and whether the care you need is available at the clinic. Bring any referral, recent dental records, medication list, and coverage details. An assessment helps determine the next step, including whether a referral is needed.
