Planning your visit

CDCP and dental insurance.

109 Dental accepts the Canadian Dental Care Plan. Bring your coverage details and ask about your estimated share of the cost before treatment.

Before your appointment

Bring your CDCP member information or private insurance details, along with photo ID. For CDCP, check that your coverage has started and is still active. Tell the office when booking that you plan to use it.

Will I have anything to pay?

CDCP does not necessarily pay the whole bill. Your share can include a co-payment, fees above the plan's reimbursement amount, or services the plan does not cover. Some treatment needs approval in advance. Ask for an estimate before agreeing to treatment.

Private plans also have their own limits and exclusions. Direct billing does not mean every charge is covered. Bring your plan details so the office can help you check.

Eligibility and applications

The Government of Canada decides CDCP eligibility. Check its current requirements and application process rather than relying on a website calculator. The clinic cannot confirm eligibility from your income alone.

Check official CDCP eligibility requirements · Apply or renew through Canada.ca

Questions to ask before treatment

  • What treatment is recommended, and what are my alternatives?
  • What is the total estimate and my expected share?
  • Does anything need preauthorization?
  • Could the estimate change, and when would we discuss that?

For the program rules, see Health Canada's coverage information and Dental Benefits Guide.

Questions about coverage

Does 109 Dental accept CDCP?

Yes. 109 Dental accepts the Canadian Dental Care Plan. Tell the office when booking and bring your member details so coverage can be checked.

Does CDCP mean my visit is free?

Not necessarily. You may have a co-payment, a difference between the clinic fee and the plan reimbursement, or charges for treatment that is not covered. Ask for an estimate before treatment.

Can I use CDCP before my coverage starts?

Check your coverage start date before booking treatment under the plan. An application or approval notice alone does not mean coverage has already started.

Does CDCP cover implants?

CDCP does not cover dental implants or implant-related procedures. Ask about the cost and any private benefits before choosing treatment.
